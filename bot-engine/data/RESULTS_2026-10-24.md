## 6. THE MOST IMPORTANT FINDING — every losing card leg had exactly 2 cards

| Match cards | Matches | Share |

| 1 | 7 | 9.3% |

**All 11 losing card legs finished on exactly 2 cards.** Not one finished on 1
or 0. The O2.5 line is a cliff: 21% of matches sit in the "just missed" bucket.

**Actual O2.5 rate across all 75 matches: 69.3%.** The model said 73.0% on the
27 referee-covered fixtures — **−2.6 points of over-confidence**, concentrated
in the high band:

| Model p2 band | n | Model said | Actual | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 51–70% | 9 | 62.8% | 77.8% | **+15.0** (under-confident) |
| 71–100% | 17 | 80.2% | 70.6% | **−9.6** (over-confident) |

**The model is too confident exactly where we leaned on it.** The high band is
over-stated by ~10 points; the mid band is under-stated by ~15. That is a
**regression-to-the-mean problem**: the model takes the referee's raw
over-3.5 rate and passes it straight through as a card probability, when the
regression should shrink it toward the mean.

**Also: the refereer's over-3.5 rate is only moderately predictive.**
`corr(referee over-3.5 rate, actual match cards) = +0.43` (n=27). Real signal,
but far weaker than the model assumed when it published 86% numbers.

### The fix (for the next card board)
1. **Shrink the refereer's rate toward the mean** before using it — the current
   regression under-shrinks at the top end.
2. **Widen the O2.5 line.** 21% of matches land on exactly 2 cards; the money
   edge, if any, is at **O1.5** (3.87 mean cards) not O2.5.
3. **Treat "referee over-3.5 rate" as one input, not the whole model.** Add
   both teams' card rates and the competition baseline.
